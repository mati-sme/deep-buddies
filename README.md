# Deep Buddies

A cozy pixel mining game for phone and desktop. You dig down a lantern-lit mine with **Pip the mole**. Grab ore before your lantern runs out, then spend the coins on a mine-cart **Upgrade Map**. Once a shaft is deep enough, a **Cave-in** gives you Relics for the **Relic Map**, where Pip evolves.

Play: https://mati-sme.github.io/deep-buddies/

## How it plays
- **Guided first dive.** A brand-new player starts straight in a short tutorial dive with no menu first. Four steps (dig down, grab the ore, open the chest, go deeper) show an arrow, keycaps or a drag hand, and each step moves on when you do it. A Skip button ends the tutorial. The first dive is laid out so it always pays enough for the first upgrades.
- **Goals.** A chain of 17 goals ("Buy your first upgrade", "Reach Stone at 25 m", ...) shows as a chip during dives and as a card in camp. Each goal points at the next thing to do. The run summary shows the goals you finished and the next one.
- **Timed runs.** Every run lasts as long as your lantern oil: 15 s at first, about 1–2 minutes later on. Oil flasks in the rock give some back. Monsters, gas pockets and lava burn oil. When the oil is gone you go back up and sell what you carried.
- **Moving and digging.** On touch screens, touch and drag anywhere on the cave: a faint floating joystick appears where your finger went down, and you walk and dig in the drag direction for as long as you hold. A short tap on a block goes and digs it. On a keyboard, use the arrow keys or WASD (dragging with the mouse works too). There are no on-screen arrow buttons. Which way you dig matters: ore veins and treasure chests are worth a lot, hard boulders slow you down, green gas rocks explode, water slows you and lava burns.
- **Pip the buddy** doesn't follow you. He wanders the explored mine on his own, digs blocks around himself (smelled ore first once he has Ore Sniffing), fights creatures near him and chatters in speech bubbles. He stays roughly within your light or a few tiles of explored tunnels, and pops back through a tunnel if he ends up far behind. From the third dive on, tap the paw button and then a spot to nudge him there. After that he goes back to exploring. Upgrades teach him Ore Sniffing (ore sparkles in the dark), Gem Hunter (he smells ore much further away), Brave Buddy (he fights) and Dynamite.
- **Cave creatures** (bats, slimes, beetles, spiders, imps, golems...) wake up when your light reaches them and bite away your oil. Your weapon hits them automatically when they get close, so swords and armor matter.
- **Upgrade Map** (52 nodes on mine-cart rails). It has six branches: Pickaxe tiers (Wooden → Heartforge), Weapon tiers (Stick → Star Saber), Lantern (oil, light, flare), Gear (boots, backpack, armor, gas mask, heat boots, helmet, gloves), Buddy, and Loot. You only ever buy upgrades. There are no helpers to buy over and over. The map opens up gradually: you see the nodes you can buy now and "?" teasers for the next ones. Coins are always whole numbers.
- **Cave-in (prestige).** Once you reach 100 m in a shaft, a cave-in resets coins, upgrades and shaft depth. In return you get Relics, roughly (depth)³. You spend them on the **Relic Map** (17 nodes), which shows a constellation over geological layers. It has permanent buffs, the Elevator, the Gear Locker, Auto-buy, and **four buddy evolutions**: Drill Pip (drills straight down), Crystal Pip (sees ore far away), Ember Pip (burns monsters, walks through lava) and Star Pip (glows in the dark).
- **Wide screens and landscape.** Landscape phones, tablets, 16:9 laptops and ultrawide monitors get a *wider* mine instead of a stretched or letterboxed one: the shaft gets more columns (11 in portrait, about 24 at 1920×1080, 32 at 2560×1080, 26 on a landscape phone, up to 40). Tile size comes from the screen height and is snapped so every art pixel is a whole number of device pixels, so the art stays crisp. If the shaft is wider than the screen, the camera scrolls sideways. Your light radius is still measured in tiles, so the dark feels the same. The camp, Upgrade Map and Relic Map use a side tab rail with side panels. On large monitors the UI scales up in proportion. Resizing and rotating the device both update the layout live, even in the middle of a run. Shaft width doesn't change pacing (see `SIM_RESULTS.md`).
- **Saves** go to localStorage on each device (autosave every 10 s, after runs and after purchases). You can move a save with export/import.

## Tech
Vanilla JS with no build step. Canvas renderer with procedural pixel art, darkness and a lantern light radius, particles and screen shake. Sound and music are made with WebAudio (no audio files). The music is a cozy 80 BPM loop in F major: the camp gets a soft pad and marimba, and dives add bass, hats and an arpeggio. Music and sound effects can be switched off separately. Icons are 10×10 pixel art written in `js/icons.js`, outlined in code; the game uses no emoji. The fonts are Pixelify Sans (OFL, subset in `fonts/`) plus a hand-made 5×7 digit font, `fonts/db-digits.woff`, built by `tools/make-digits-font.py`. `tools/cover/` draws the store covers in code (`node tools/cover/render-covers.js <outdir>`). It is an installable PWA: `sw.js` caches the game cache-first, and its `CACHE` name must be bumped on every release.

- `js/data.js` holds the strata, ores, creatures, upgrade map and relic map. `js/core.js` holds the economy and the world simulation, with no DOM, so Node can use it too.
- `node sim/pacing.js [minutesPerDay] [maxDays]` runs a pacing simulation: a bot plays real runs through the same `World` class the game uses. Results are in `SIM_RESULTS.md`.
- `node test/e2e.js [url]` is a headless Chrome test. In portrait (mobile and desktop) it tests the guided first dive, that there is no emoji in the UI, that music is playing, whole-number coins, the gradual map reveal, goals, migrating a v1 save, the touch joystick, taps, the keyboard and mouse-drag, a full run, buying an upgrade, a cave-in, buying a relic node, and that the save survives a reload. In landscape (1920×1080, 2560×1080, and an 844×390 phone) it checks that the layout fits without overflow, the full-width shaft, whole-pixel tiles, keyboard or joystick play, rotating mid-run, and the Upgrade Map and Relic Map. `node test/shot-wide.js [url]` takes the landscape screenshots.
